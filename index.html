The explosion 49M Instagram views in four months

One backyard. Four months. The whole world pressed play.

In June 2026 Amitai Kurtz posted a Chopin nocturne played as samba. Within days it was on feeds in Brazil, the US, Japan and across Europe. Then came Mozart with a horn section, and it passed 17 million views.

Every new session landed bigger. Swan Lake passed 8 million, Beethoven and Bach passed millions each, and the comment sections kept asking for one thing in every language: the album.

It started as a way to bring people to a solo album. It became the project: a camera, a red keyboard and arrangements good enough to travel.

The programme

But He Was Born inBrazil

Beethoven's Fifth opens with a rhythm, not a melody: short, short, short, long. The album plays it as what it always was, a percussion figure. Eight tracks, seventeen minutes, recorded in the studio with an orchestra. The catalogue numbers are the composers', the rhythm section is Rio's.

The arranger

Amitai Kurtz hears both traditions from the inside

Amitai Kurtz is an Israeli composer, arranger, producer and multi-instrumentalist. He studied music in Brazil, and the samba feel he brought home has run through everything he has played since.

He was working on a solo album when he started reimagining well-known pieces as samba, filmed with friends in a newly renovated backyard. The first one was a Chopin nocturne. Within weeks the videos were circulating worldwide, and the project took over.

He writes every arrangement down to the last percussion hit, which is how a run of videos turned into a full orchestral record in about two months.
