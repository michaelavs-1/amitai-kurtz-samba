Amitai Kurtz Samba Project · The album Beethoven, Mozart, Chopin.
But he was born in Brazil

Out now on every platform. Eight pieces of the classical canon, arranged, produced and orchestrated by Amitai Kurtz, played as samba and bossa nova.

Listen to the album Watch the sessions

Beethoven's Fifth opens with a rhythm, not a melody. Four notes, three of them on the same pitch. This album plays it as what it always was: a percussion figure.

The repertoire is the most familiar music in the Western canon. The four notes everyone can hum, the piece every piano student has played, the swans, the Turkish march. Amitai Kurtz finds the groove that was already inside each one and lets it move.

It started as a set of videos filmed in a backyard. They travelled across Brazil, the US, Japan and Europe, and the comment sections kept asking for the same thing in every language. The album is the answer, with a studio, an orchestra and horns recorded in Brazil.

The arranger

Amitai Kurtz hears both traditions from the inside

Amitai Kurtz is an Israeli composer, arranger, producer and multi-instrumentalist. He studied music in Brazil, and the samba feel he brought home has run through everything he has played since.

He was working on a solo album when he started reimagining well-known pieces as samba, filmed with friends in a newly renovated backyard. The first one was a Chopin nocturne. Within weeks the videos were circulating worldwide, and the project took over.

He writes every arrangement down to the last percussion hit, which is how a run of videos turned into a full orchestral record in about two months.
